# Prioritization rubric

How the scanner orders open loops, most-urgent-first. The board renders this order directly,
and the "Right now" hero is simply the top item.

Tweak this file to change what "urgent" means to you.

## The tiers

**Urgent** — act within 24–48h or something breaks.
- Hard deadlines (applications, payments, renewals).
- Time-sensitive prep (an interview tomorrow, a meeting in an hour).
- Anything with a cost for waiting (a trial about to renew, a slot about to fill).

**High** — act this week.
- Active projects with momentum: keep them moving while they're warm.
- Commitments to other people (replies owed, follow-ups promised).
- `waiting` items whose nudge date arrived.

**Normal** — real, but no clock.
- Ongoing projects, drafts in progress, plans forming.
- The bulk of a healthy board lives here.

**Low** — the backlog.
- Nice-to-haves, someday/maybe, research rabbit holes.
- Never deleted, never nagged about. Visible at the bottom so they stop occupying headspace.

## Tie-breakers (same tier)

1. Earlier `due` date first.
2. External commitment beats self-commitment (someone waiting on you outranks you waiting on you).
3. Smaller next action first — a 5-minute win beats a 5-hour project when energy is scarce.

## Anti-rules

- Don't inflate: when everything is urgent, nothing is. Most items are normal; that's fine.
- Don't age items up automatically. An old normal item is still normal unless a deadline appeared.
- The user's explicit priority beats the rubric. If they said "this can wait," it waits.
